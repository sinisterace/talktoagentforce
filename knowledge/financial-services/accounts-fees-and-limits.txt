ACCOUNTS, FEES, AND LIMITS

This document covers everyday checking and savings accounts: the fees that
can apply, how to avoid them, and the daily limits on moving money. Every
amount here is written in words on purpose, because this text is read aloud.

MONTHLY MAINTENANCE FEE

Everyday Checking has a monthly maintenance fee of twelve dollars. It is
waived in any statement period where the account keeps a minimum daily
balance of fifteen hundred dollars, or receives a direct deposit of five
hundred dollars or more. Meeting either one waives the fee for that period,
so most customers with a regular paycheck never pay it.

Savings has no monthly maintenance fee.

OVERDRAFT

An overdraft fee of thirty-four dollars applies when a transaction posts for
more than the available balance and the bank pays it anyway. There is a
courtesy threshold: if the account is overdrawn by five dollars or less at
the end of the day, no overdraft fee is charged. There is a limit of three
overdraft fees per day. Setting up overdraft protection from a linked
savings account avoids the fee entirely by transferring the shortfall
instead.

ATM AND FOREIGN TRANSACTIONS

There is no fee at in-network ATMs. Using an out-of-network ATM is three
dollars per withdrawal, plus whatever the ATM owner charges. Purchases in a
foreign currency carry a foreign transaction fee of three percent of the
amount.

WIRE TRANSFERS

Domestic wires sent before four o'clock Eastern on a business day go out the
same day. International wires have a cutoff of two o'clock Eastern. A wire
requested after the cutoff, or on a weekend or holiday, goes out the next
business day.

DAILY LIMITS

The daily limits on Everyday Checking are five thousand dollars in debit
card purchases, one thousand dollars in ATM withdrawals, and ten thousand
dollars in external transfers. These reset at midnight Eastern. A limit can
be raised temporarily for something like a large purchase by calling in.

SAVINGS WITHDRAWALS

Savings allows six withdrawals or outgoing transfers per statement cycle.
Beyond that the bank may decline the transfer or, on repeated cycles, move
the account to checking. Deposits and ATM withdrawals do not count toward
the six.

STATEMENTS

Statements are issued monthly. Paper statements are two dollars per cycle;
electronic statements are free and are the default.
