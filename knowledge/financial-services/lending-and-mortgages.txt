LENDING AND MORTGAGES

This document covers what a loan application needs, the stages it moves
through, and how rate locks work. Amounts and timelines are written in words
because this text is read aloud.

DOCUMENTS A MORTGAGE NEEDS

A mortgage application is reviewed against a standard checklist: the two most
recent pay stubs, the last two years of W-2 forms or tax returns, the last
two months of bank statements, and a government-issued photo ID. Someone who
is self-employed provides two years of tax returns and a year-to-date profit
and loss statement instead of pay stubs. Missing one item is the most common
reason an application sits waiting, and it is usually a single document like
a current pay stub.

DOCUMENTS AN AUTO LOAN NEEDS

An auto loan needs proof of income, a valid driver's license, and proof of
insurance for the vehicle. If the vehicle is already chosen, the purchase
agreement or the dealer's buyer's order completes the file.

PRE-QUALIFICATION VERSUS PRE-APPROVAL

Pre-qualification is an early estimate based on what you tell the bank, and
it does not pull a hard credit report. Pre-approval is a firmer commitment
based on verified income and a credit check, and it is what sellers take
seriously. Pre-approval generally holds for ninety days.

UNDERWRITING STAGES

An application moves through submission, document review, underwriting, and
finally a decision. In underwriting an underwriter verifies income and
assets, orders an appraisal on a mortgage, and confirms the file meets the
loan guidelines. Once every outstanding document is in, underwriting a
straightforward file takes about three business days.

APPRAISAL AND TITLE

On a mortgage the appraisal confirms the property is worth the loan amount,
and a title search confirms there are no liens. An appraisal that comes in
low, or a title issue like an unresolved lien, is a common cause of delay and
is handled by the loan team, not by the applicant.

RATE LOCKS

A rate lock holds your interest rate while the loan is processed, protecting
you if rates rise. A standard lock runs thirty, forty-five, or sixty days. If
closing runs past the lock, an extension is usually available for a fee of
about one quarter of one percent of the loan amount. If the lock expires
before closing, the rate is reset to the current market rate.

CLOSING COSTS

Closing costs on a mortgage typically run two to five percent of the loan
amount and cover the appraisal, title work, origination, and prepaid taxes
and insurance. A loan estimate itemizing these is provided within three
business days of applying.
