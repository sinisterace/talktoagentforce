EMERGENCY RESPONSE AND SAFETY POLICY

WHEN TO STOP AND CALL 911 OR THE GAS UTILITY

The following conditions are not service calls. They are emergencies that require
the fire department or the gas utility before any technician is dispatched. In all
of these cases the correct instruction is: leave the building immediately, take
everyone with you, and call 911 or the gas utility's emergency line from outside.
Do not use light switches, do not use a phone inside the building, and do not try
to shut anything off yourself.

- Any smell of gas, including a faint or intermittent rotten-egg or sulfur smell.
- A carbon monoxide detector that is alarming or has alarmed recently.
- Symptoms consistent with carbon monoxide exposure in more than one person or
  pet in the building: headache, dizziness, nausea, confusion, unusual sleepiness
  that improves when outdoors.
- Smoke or a burning smell from any part of the heating system.
- Visible flame outside the furnace's combustion chamber, or flame rollout.
- Sparking, arcing, or a scorched-plastic smell from an electrical panel or from
  the equipment's wiring.
- Water actively contacting live electrical components.

Do not schedule an appointment while any of these conditions are active. Once the
fire department or the utility has cleared the building, we can dispatch a
technician for the repair.

WHAT COUNTS AS AN HVAC EMERGENCY

These are urgent service calls and are dispatched ahead of scheduled maintenance:

- No heat when the outdoor temperature is below 40 degrees Fahrenheit, or any no
  heat call involving an infant, an elderly occupant, or someone with a medical
  condition.
- No cooling when the outdoor temperature is above 90 degrees Fahrenheit, or any
  no cooling call involving a vulnerable occupant.
- A water leak from the equipment that is actively damaging the building.
- A frozen or burst pipe associated with a hydronic heating system.
- Total loss of climate control in a commercial space that holds temperature
  sensitive inventory, a server room, or a medical or food service operation.

These are not emergencies and are scheduled normally: a thermostat that needs
programming, a noisy but functioning unit, a routine filter change, a slow drain,
a request for a quote, and a system that is merely less efficient than expected.

RESPONSE TARGETS

Emergency calls are dispatched to the nearest available technician. Typical
arrival is within two to four hours during business hours. After hours, overnight,
and on holidays, typical arrival is within four hours. Comfort Care Plus and
Commercial Priority members are placed at the front of the queue. If no technician
covers the caller's area, we take the details and a dispatcher calls back with the
soonest available option rather than promising a window we cannot meet.

AFTER-HOURS SURCHARGE

Emergency service outside 7 AM to 7 PM local time on weekdays, or at any time on
weekends and holidays, carries a $145 after-hours dispatch surcharge. This
surcharge is waived for Comfort Care Plus and Commercial Priority members. The
surcharge is separate from the diagnostic fee and from any repair cost.

WHAT WE ASK FOR ON AN EMERGENCY CALL

A name, a callback number, the service address including postal code, whether the
property is residential or commercial, and a plain description of what the system
is doing or not doing. We also always screen for the gas, carbon monoxide, smoke,
and electrical conditions listed at the top of this document before booking
anything.
